Meet the HazSight device

Driver drowsiness is one of the major factors behind unsafe driving. HazSight watches the driver’s face in real time, and when it sees signs of drowsiness it warns the driver and lights up a hazard light so nearby drivers know to give space.

Prolonged eye closure

Detected within 1.0 second of the eyes staying shut.

Yawning

Prolonged yawning, sustained for 1.5 seconds, is treated as a sign of drowsiness.

No face detected

If the driver’s face is absent from the camera, HazSight responds within 3 seconds.

  • Camera watches the driver
  • Drowsiness is detected
  • Driver is alerted
  • Hazard light turns on wirelessly

Built on a Raspberry Pi 4 with an IR-Cut OV5647 camera, using Python, OpenCV and MediaPipe, with IoT-enabled wireless hazard-light activation.

Abstract

Road safety remains a major concern worldwide, as road traffic crashes continue to cause substantial loss of life and injury. One major factor contributing to unsafe driving is driver drowsiness. This study developed the HazSight device, a Raspberry Pi 4-based driver drowsiness detection system with Internet of Things IoT-enabled wireless hazard-light activation designed to alert drivers and nearby road users. The system uses an IR-Cut OV5647 camera with Python, OpenCV, and MediaPipe to detect prolonged eye closure, yawning and the absence of a face.

The HazSight device was evaluated for its feature’s performance, hazard light activation for nearby-driver recognition and its general acceptability. The results show that the device demonstrated continuous real-time monitoring of the driver’s facial condition through automatic activation of an external hazard light upon detecting drowsiness. The device was evaluated with 96% accuracy rate in alerting drivers and external hazard light activation. The accuracy of the device achieved 100% accuracy rate in closed-eye detection within 1.0 second and in no-face detection within 3 seconds, while the accuracy in yawn detection sustained for 1.5 seconds registered an 86% accuracy rate. The HazSight device is highly reliable in recognizing prolonged yawning as an indicator of drowsiness.

The general acceptability of the device was found to be highly reliable, user-friendly and highly functional. It demonstrated a comparable accuracy with selected existing systems while its nearby-driver warning capability could not be directly compared. The HazSight device showed a relatively lower development cost, while maintaining comparable detection performance.
